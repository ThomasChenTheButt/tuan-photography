Openings

Sixteen ways the home page could begin. Open one; it plays once and the page is itself again. Add ?opening=name to the home page's address to see any of them.

  1. ApertureA shutter blink, then the iris blades turn open from the centre while the focus pulls sharp.
  2. Contact sheetEvery photograph you have made, laid out as one contact sheet on black. One frame is chosen and fills the window.
  3. Film stripA strip of film races past with its sprocket holes, slows, and stops on the one frame.
  4. BurstHard cuts between full-window photographs, faster and faster, like a burst, until it holds on the one.
  5. LettersYour name on paper, the photograph showing only through the letters. The letters grow until one stroke is the whole window.
  6. ReelYour own footage: four Barcelona clips cut hard, one after another, then the still.
  7. OrbitThe earth from space, turning from Taiwan toward New Zealand, falling until the land is the photograph.
  8. RouteA pen line draws every journey you have made across a paper map, a small print at each place, then the map dives into the last one.
  9. Scroll to openNothing plays by itself. A small print on the paper; your scroll opens it until it is the window.
  10. BookA closed guidebook on the paper. The cover swings open; the first plate is the photograph, and you move into it.
  11. StackPhotographs pop up at the centre one after another, each landing on the last, until the final one spreads to the edges.
  12. CounterThe award-site classic: a number counts to one hundred on paper, then the photograph wipes up from below.
  13. SlicesThe photograph in tall slices that rise one after another and close into one frame.
  14. RippleHeat haze over the frame, the photograph bending like air over hot ground, settling to still and sharp.
  15. Fly throughA camera flies through one photograph into the next, each nested inside the last, and arrives at the opening one.
  16. Pull backThe first try: the page arrives on the summit and pulls back until the whole photograph is there.
