## 1. typohypergraphicobject (James Langdon's book site)
- URL: https://www.typohypergraphicobject.page/
- Source: Hoverstates; confirmed in the live Astro CSS and inline JS.
- What (read from code, not watched): (1) On a white page the title arrives one word at a time, typo / hyper / graphic / object, each 220 ms after the last, each starting as an outlined, transparent word and filling to solid black. (2) Every photograph of the book sits under a clone of itself; the clone is shown wrongly inked (brightness 69 %, hue rotated 200°, saturation 345 %, contrast 157 %), like a mis-registered proof. When the figure scrolls into view the clone's filter relaxes to none in about a third of the base time while the true image fades up beneath over 1.7× the base time, so the picture settles from false colour to true colour instead of fading in from nothing. (3) Long quotations are written word by word as the visitor scrolls.
- Why it grabs: nothing fades in; everything arrives the way print does, words inked, pictures coming into register. It reads as a proof coming off the press, not a page loading. Adjacent to, but not, the cut "darkroom" (which develops from black with grain): this is offset printing, not a tray.
- Quality to borrow: a photograph arriving by colour settling (false plate to true plate), not by opacity.

## 2. Iceberg, a transmedia documentary (Plastic Design)
- URL: https://icebergdoc.org/
- Source: Awwwards Loading page collection ("Loading" by Plastic Design); confirmed in the live Nuxt chunk (Intro component, GSAP + drawSVG).
- What (read from code): (1) 0 to 2.2 s: black; the full-window film is clipped to a zero-height polygon at the top edge and opens downward to the full frame, power4.inOut, like a blind. (2) From 0.6 s, 3 s long: the word ICEBERG stacked three times splits into characters that climb out of the frame (-300 %, 0.07 s stagger), as if the word rose up through its own copies; two copies then vanish and the third resets in place. (3) 1.5 s: header fades in. 2 s, 1 s long: a hand-written sentence in hairline SVG strokes writes itself (drawSVG). (4) Looping forever: five hairline (stroke 0.2) hand-drawn landscape lines draw themselves one at a time over the moving film, each 1.4 to 2.6 s power4.inOut, erased before the next, random pause between. (5) On scroll, pinned and scrubbed: a black overlay cut into six vertical strips scales in with a stagger and closes over the film like a slatted curtain.
- Why it grabs: the film is never captioned or buttoned; the only things over it are pencil-thin lines drawing themselves at pencil speed, in their own order, so the frame feels alive and hand-annotated. The top-down blind is one decisive move.
- Flag: lines drawn over the picture brush against the excluded "circled spot with a line" family and rule 1 if put on the photograph itself; the quality stands on paper beside it.
- Quality to borrow: a hairline that draws itself slowly as the only life in the frame (or, second choice, the frame opening as a blind from the top edge).

## 3. 100 Years of Columbia Pictures (Watson Design Group)
- URL: https://columbia100.watson.la/
- Source: Awwwards inspiration search 'intro' (Site of the Day + Developer Award, 2024-04-18); confirmed in the live Nuxt entry bundle (Intro component, one GSAP timeline, 8 s, click to skip).
- What (read from code): (1) 0 to 3 s: from black, the brandmark (the torch lady) fades in. (2) 0 to 8 s: the scene is separate planes moving at different rates: clouds scale 1 to 1.6 drifting up and left; the lady scales 1.6 to 1.1 rising; a glow behind her scales 5 to 1 and dies in the last second; the whole brandmark starts at scale 2 and over the full 8 s pulls back and travels up into the exact size and position of its slot in the wordmark (measured from the DOM), its mask tightening from 150 % to 100 %. (3) 4 to 6 s: the torch flame fades in only at the fourth second. (4) 6 to 8 s: the wordmark settles from 1.35 to 1; an outlined "100" stroked in a gold gradient sits in the mask. Then the landing view replaces it.
- Why it grabs: it is the studio ident before a film, built from DOM layers: one flat picture made deep by cutting it into planes that move at different speeds, with a light that comes on late. A stranger waits for the torch.
- Flag: it is a pull-back, which the owner found boring on its own ("pullback" survivor); what is new is the multiplane depth and the late event.
- Quality to borrow: multi-plane depth from one still image (sky, figure, foreground at different rates) with one late event that rewards waiting.

## 4. Theater im Depot, Dortmund
- URL: https://theaterimdepot.de/
- Source: Hoverstates ("the website presenting a window to the content inside a larger menu setting"); confirmed in the live HTML, CSS and script.js (KUTE path morph).
- What (read from code): (1) The home page loads already behind a white flat covering the whole window: an SVG mask whose hole is a hand-drawn ellipse, a proscenium, with the flat's edge drawn as a thick black line (drop-shadow 0.18 to 0.5 vw), so the content is seen through an oval stage opening; menu, logo, social and legal lines are the set around it. (2) On the visitor's first click anywhere, the ellipse morphs over 1 s (cubic out) into a second, larger hand-drawn shape and the flat slides down out of the window (0.5 s linear); 300 ms later the header opens. (3) The page is now the full window; the flat waits at the bottom (10 vh "cover") and the opening never replays.
- Why it grabs: the whole page is a window inside a bigger drawn stage set, and the visitor raises the curtain with one click. No preloader number, no fade: a proscenium that gives way.
- Quality to borrow: the opening is a hand-drawn hole in a flat that the visitor opens with the first click, with the content already there behind it.

## 5. ANORAK Film, Berlin
- URL: https://anorakfilm.com/
- Source: Hoverstates ("a simple text-based landing page, then drifts into a dreamlike text fade/bleed effect using variable fonts"); confirmed in the live SvelteKit CSS and page module.
- What (read from code): (1) No picture at all: the landing is one justified paragraph in a variable grotesk (ABC Monument Grotesk) at weight 250, a long nonsense poem ("The speed of dark? Half of 0?") in which each director's name is a link. (2) Before the paragraph is shown the script measures every name at weight 800 and again at 350 and stores both widths; names are set to 350 with compensating margins, so a name can swell to 800 without the justified lines reflowing (0.25 s linear on font-variation-settings and letter-spacing). (3) As the visitor scrolls, scroll position is mapped onto the list of directors and one name at a time is marked active, alternating on and off, so names pulse bold through the paragraph one after another; on desktop a hover does the same; the active director's teaser film appears in a fixed overlay behind a 2.5 s backdrop blur. (4) The rest of the site is hard cuts and fast transitions.
- Why it grabs: the only motion is weight. A page of grey text, then a name goes black and heavy, then another. Dreamlike, and nothing slides or fades.
- Quality to borrow: motion by typographic weight alone, a variable font swelling in place without reflow, as the whole opening gesture.

## 6. Fog Line (Larry Gottheim, 1970)
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fog_Line (also https://movingimageartists.co.uk/2019/11/03/collection2-of-fog-mist-moisture/)
- Source: a search for openings that start on a veiled landscape; read from the Wikipedia entry and the Moving Image Artists essay, not watched.
- What (from descriptions): (1) One static shot, eleven minutes, silent, telephoto. From the first frame the landscape is there but under heavy fog; only two telephone wires crossing the frame are certain. (2) The fog moves sideways across the frame and thins unevenly, so the picture arrives in patches: a tree, then the field, then the horizon line that gives distance and scale. (3) Two horses walk in and graze, a bird crosses. (4) It ends when the fog has gone and the landscape is simply there. Nothing is added to the frame at any point; Gottheim said he just looked through the viewfinder and let it unfold by itself.
- Why it grabs: the picture is never brought in; it is uncovered by weather. A stranger stays because the frame is changing without anything being done to it, and the moment the mountain is fully there is hard to name. Paul Schrader: it shows how magical waiting can be.
- Flag: brushes "exposure" (white coming down) and "ripple" (soft distortion settling). It is neither: the tones of the picture never change and nothing is bent; a white veil that is thick in places and thin in others drifts off. For his site it would be four seconds, not eleven minutes, and Aoraki is a mountain that really does hide in cloud.
- Quality to borrow: the photograph is present from the first frame and arrives by a veil lifting unevenly and sideways, like weather, not by opacity, exposure or focus.

## 7. The rangefinder patch coming into coincidence (Leica M)
- URL: https://glass.photo/photography-terms/rangefinder (also https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangefinder_camera)
- Source: a camera mechanism, not a website; read from the glass.photo term page and Wikipedia.
- What (from descriptions): (1) Through a rangefinder's finder the scene is seen whole and sharp, with a small bright patch at the centre that shows a second image of the same scene. (2) Out of focus, the patch holds two copies of the scene offset sideways, a doubled edge of every vertical: a door frame, a lamp post, a ridge. (3) Turning the focus ring slides the second image across the first; the photographer watches a chosen vertical edge close its gap. (4) When the two images merge into one the subject is in focus and the doubling is gone; nothing else in the picture has changed.
- Why it grabs: focus is shown as two pictures sliding into one, a motion anyone can read without knowing cameras, with a precise snap at the end. It is the one focusing gesture that is not a blur.
- Flag: a second copy of the photograph lies over the photograph until the last instant, which the router may read against rule 1, though it is not a word, number, logo or button, and at rest only the single picture remains. It is adjacent to "aperture" (which pulls focus by blur) but is displacement, not blur; and to the cut "ripple" only in that something settles. Buildable with two copies of the same image and one transform.
- Quality to borrow: arrival by coincidence: two offset copies of the picture slide into register and become one, the way a rangefinder finds focus, with no blur at all.

## 8. The Kodak Carousel slide change (and Mad Men, "The Wheel", 2007)
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carousel_slide_projector (the scene: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wheel_(Mad_Men))
- Source: the projector's mechanism from Wikipedia; the scene from the episode entry. Read, not watched here, though the scene is widely known.
- What (from descriptions): (1) A dark room; the projector's lamp throws a warm empty rectangle, or nothing, because a shutter inside the projector closes during a slide change. (2) The mechanism lifts the previous slide up into the tray, the tray turns one notch, and the next slide drops by gravity into the gate between lamp and lens: one mechanical beat, about a second, with the clunk everyone remembers. (3) The shutter opens and the photograph is simply there, full frame, lit from behind, slightly soft until the focus is touched. (4) In "The Wheel" the slides are Don Draper's own family photographs, advanced one by one in a darkened room, and the pitch is that the device is not a wheel but a carousel that takes us back; one listener leaves in tears.
- Why it grabs: the picture arrives as an object falling into place, not as a fade; the dark beat before it and the mechanical certainty of the drop make a stranger wait for the next one. It is the twentieth century's way of showing a photograph to a room.
- Flag: brushes "prints" (physical prints dealt onto paper) and "stack" (pictures popping up one after another). It is one frame, dropped from above into a fixed gate on a dark stage, with no scaling and no pile; the exit of the previous slide upward and the drop of the next is a single rhythm. If several slides are shown it becomes "burst" with a drop instead of a cut, so the borrowing should be one drop.
- Quality to borrow: a dark beat, then the photograph drops whole into the frame by gravity and lands with a mechanical settle, lit from behind.

## 9. Printing plates coming into register (offset printing; the web sighting is Hoverstates on typohypergraphicobject.page)
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_registration (the web sighting: https://hoverstat.es/ entry for www.typohypergraphicobject.page, 28 Nov 2025)
- Source: the print shop, not a website; Hoverstates describes the James Langdon book site's image load-in as feeling like the layered print process, which is what pointed here. Read, not watched.
- What (from descriptions): (1) A colour photograph on a press is printed as separate plates, one ink each, laid on the sheet one after another. (2) While the plates are out of register the picture shows colour fringes and ghost edges: a cyan ridge beside the real ridge, a magenta sky slightly east of the blue one; the picture is readable but "fuzzy, blurred or out of register". (3) The pressman adjusts until the registration marks on the plates and the sheet coincide. (4) In register, the fringes vanish and the photograph is simply correct; nothing was added, the layers only met.
- Why it grabs: the picture assembles from its own inks, each arriving a little wrong and then meeting. It is the one arrival that belongs to a printed journal rather than to a screen, and the instant of register is a visible click.
- Flag: kin to my first-round find 1 (colour settling by hue), but a different mechanism: there the colour of one image relaxed; here three or four separations slide into place. It can be mistaken for the "RGB split" glitch of award sites if it jitters; done as plates it moves once, slowly, and settles. Buildable offline: channel separations by an SVG feColorMatrix filter on copies of the same image, blended with mix-blend-mode and offset by a transform, no assets to cut.
- Quality to borrow: the photograph arrives as its own colour plates landing one by one and sliding into register, the material of a printed page.

## 10. The Sopranos main title (1999)
- URL: https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/the-sopranos/
- Source: Art of the Title's write-up with the makers; read, and the sequence is well known.
- What (from description): (1) From the first frame we are already moving: the view is from the driver's seat, out through the windscreen, with the car's own frame around it. (2) The driver is never shown whole, only eyes in the mirror, a hand on the wheel, a cigar, a shoulder; the city passes in pieces, tunnel, tolls, smokestacks, signage, then trees and rock. (3) The credits wipe on and off to the side, moving with the car rather than sitting still on the picture. (4) It ends when the car comes up the driveway and stops at the house: the journey's last frame is the place.
- Why it grabs: the opening is an arrival. Nothing is revealed; we travel and then stop, and the stopping is the event. His lead photograph is already this frame: a landscape through a car window.
- Flag: a travelling picture is a camera move, and the router has rejected pullback and flythrough. This is neither in nor out but a sideways travel that decelerates to rest, the picture stopping in the frame the way a car stops; at phone width a cover-fitted landscape is wider than the window, so the travel can be real there and only a short settle at desktop width. If any word is used it would wipe on and off beside the picture, on the stage, never across it.
- Quality to borrow: the cover is reached by stopping: a sideways travel that slows and comes to rest exactly on the photograph, arrival rather than reveal.

## 11. Peel-apart instant film (Polaroid pack film, Fuji FP-100C)
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant_film (the practice: https://petapixel.com/2021/02/13/how-to-make-a-negative-from-your-instant-polaroid-prints)
- Source: a photographic process, not a website; read from the Wikipedia entry and PetaPixel, not watched.
- What (from descriptions): (1) The photographer pulls a tab and the exposed sheet is drawn out of the camera through two rollers, which "spread a reagent between the two layers" so negative and positive leave the camera as one sealed sandwich, picture side hidden. (2) Nothing visible happens for about a minute (90 seconds for FP-100C): the photographer holds a blank paper envelope while the picture develops inside it. (3) Then, in one motion from a corner, the negative sheet is peeled away and "the picture which was transferred to the positive receiving sheet" is simply there, finished, still wet-looking. (4) The peeled negative, chemicals on it, is thrown away; it darkens to black within seconds.
- Why it grabs: the whole suspense is in a blank sheet, and the picture is delivered by one pull of the hand, complete, with no fade and no development visible. Everyone who has seen it done remembers the peel. The paper that comes away is exactly the material of a printed journal.
- Flag: not "negative" (a colour negative flashing to positive) nor "prints" (prints dealt onto paper): here the negative is the lid and leaves the frame, and there is one print. Kin to Fog Line only in that something covers the picture, but the clearing is one decisive diagonal peel. Buildable: a clip-path polygon driven from one corner plus a drawn curl shadow.
- Quality to borrow: a blank sheet peeled off diagonally in one pull, the picture already finished beneath it.

## 12. The focal-plane shutter's first curtain (Leica, 18 milliseconds)
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focal-plane_shutter (seen slowed down: https://petapixel.com/2015/01/29/exploring-dslr-shutters-work-10000-frames-per-second/)
- Source: a camera mechanism; read from the Wikipedia entry and PetaPixel's account of the Slow Mo Guys' 10,000 fps footage. Not watched here.
- What (from descriptions): (1) In the dark behind the lens the film gate is covered by the first curtain, an opaque rubberised cloth. (2) On release the curtain crosses the 36 mm gate in 18 milliseconds, "at 2 meters per second", one straight edge travelling right to left, and the picture exists only in its wake. (3) At slow speeds the gate stands fully open for a beat; at fast speeds the second curtain is already chasing, so the frame is exposed through "a vertical slit that travels horizontally across the film" and no part of the picture is ever seen whole in the camera. (4) Slowed ten thousand times, the Slow Mo Guys show the mirror flip, the curtain edge sweeping, the sensor lit, the second curtain closing: one mechanical sentence.
- Why it grabs: it is the actual instant a photograph is made, and nobody has seen it with their eyes. A single hard edge sweeping the frame at machine speed is the opposite of a fade; the picture is behind it the whole time.
- Flag: a wipe, so the router may read it beside "slices" (many tall strips rising) or the dark edges of "aperture"; it is one edge, one direction, one pass, with the rest of the mechanism (mirror, second curtain) left out. The rolling-shutter stretch of moving subjects is a texture to avoid, not borrow.
- Quality to borrow: one opaque edge sweeping the frame once at mechanical speed, the picture present behind it from the first instant.

## 13. The tissue guard of a photobook plate (L'Épreuve Photographique, 1904)
- URL: https://photoseed.com/collection/single/tissue-guard-marine-hollandaise/ (and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glassine)
- Source: a printed object in PhotoSeed's collection; read from its catalogue page, not handled.
- What (from description): (1) In the photogravure folios of 1900 to 1904 each plate is laid under a sheet of thin tissue, "glued along the entire length of the left-hand margin of the plate", so it hinges like a page. (2) The tissue is printed in letterpress with the publication's name at the upper left, the plate number at the top right, and the photographer's name and the picture's title at the lower right, all in the plate's margin, not over the image. (3) Through the tissue the photograph is already visible, softened and paled, as if under breath on glass. (4) The reader turns the tissue to the left in one motion and the plate is clear; the tissue lies against the facing page and the picture is the only thing on the sheet.
- Why it grabs: it is the ceremony of a serious photobook, a veil you lift yourself; the photograph is the same before and after, only the air between changes, and the change happens at the speed of a hand. It is the Fog Line quality with the one thing Fog Line lacked: a single, authored clearing.
- Flag: while the tissue is down its printed words lie between the eye and the photograph; the originals keep them in the margin, and on a full-window picture the margin would have to be a paper strip at the edge of the stage, or the words left off. Kin to scout 3's Yosemite Book (leaves turning) but this is one translucent leaf, not a sequence of pages, and nothing grows or moves but the tissue. Buildable: a paper-white layer at partial opacity with a 3D rotateY about its left edge, Web Animations API, one ease.
- Quality to borrow: a translucent leaf hinged on one edge, the picture visible through it from the start, cleared by a single turn.

## 14. The match cut in Lawrence of Arabia (Anne V. Coates, 1962)
- URL: https://www.openculture.com/2019/09/the-greatest-cut-in-film-history.html (scene notes also at https://www.filmsite.org/lawr.html)
- Source: the film's editor, through Open Culture's write-up and Filmsite's scene notes; read, and the cut is well known.
- What (from descriptions): (1) An office in Cairo: Lawrence strikes a match and lights Dryden's cigarette, holds the flame, and then, filling the frame in close-up, blows it out. (2) At the instant the flame dies the film cuts, with no dissolve, to the desert horizon at dawn, the sun's rim just rising, the frame almost black beneath an orange line. (3) The shot holds while the sun lifts clear of the horizon, far longer than any shot before it; the small flame has become the largest light there is. (4) The script called for a dissolve; Coates and Lean saw the direct cut in the cutting room, and Lean sent it back to be made "perfect", which meant removing two frames.
- Why it grabs: the whole landscape arrives in a single frame, on a cue given by a small act a second earlier, and then nothing is added for a long time. The leap in scale (a match to the sun) is what the audience remembers; the restraint afterward is what makes it land.
- Flag: a hard cut, which the router reads as "burst" or "reel" when it repeats; this is one cut, prepared by a gesture and answered by a graphic match (the flame's shape and colour returned at full size by the picture), followed by a long hold. The small thing on paper that the photograph answers would be the authored part; a mountain through a car window has its own candidates (a cloud, a smear on glass, a sun).
- Quality to borrow: one hard cut to the full picture on a cue, where a small shape on the paper is answered at full size by the photograph, then a long hold.

## 15. The light table striking (a fluorescent light box with a transparency on it)
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_table (the lamp's start: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescent_lamp#Starting)
- Source: a photographer's tool, read from the Wikipedia entries; the behaviour is familiar to anyone who has used a preheat-ballast light box.
- What (from descriptions): (1) A dark room; on the unlit glass of the light table lies a large transparency, near black, its image barely legible by the room's light. (2) The switch is thrown. A preheat fluorescent tube does not simply come on: the starter heats the electrodes, the tube flashes and fails once or twice, a stutter of pale light that shows the picture for a frame and takes it away. (3) Then the arc strikes and holds, and the tube comes to full brightness within about a second; the transparency, lit from behind, is suddenly the brightest thing in the room, colours fully saturated, the way a slide looks only on a light box. (4) Nothing else is on the glass; the photographer leans in with a loupe.
- Why it grabs: a dark screen that stutters twice and then holds steady is a sound and a rhythm people know from every corridor and darkroom; the stutter is the authored moment and the steady light is the arrival. A transparency on a light box is the one way a photograph is seen glowing rather than reflecting.
- Flag: light coming up is kin to the cut "exposure", which came down from white by stops, and to "darkroom", which developed from black; this is neither a ramp nor a development but a strike, two false starts and one hold, with the picture complete at every flash. Scout 3's Reuben Wu is light travelling across the land; this is the whole picture lit at once from behind. Buildable: brightness on the picture driven by a stepped keyframe, no asset.
- Quality to borrow: the picture lit from behind by a lamp that strikes with two false flashes and then holds, the stutter being the only motion.

## 20. The match-needle meter (Pentax Spotmatic, 1964): a needle swings, is brought to centre, and the shutter fires

- Flag: kin to ripple (heat-haze distortion settling to sharp, excluded) and to projector's mechanical settle; the difference is no distortion of the picture and no easing, only a rigid shiver of the whole frame that stops. A modest effect: the risk is that it reads as a glitch, so the half-second of blur in the shiver and the clean stop have to be exact; reduced-motion skips it.
- Quality to borrow: the picture arrives by going still, a tremor of the whole frame that stops in one beat.

## 28. Aoraki's cloud: the cloud drifts off the summit and out of the frame
- URL: https://thewanderinglens.com/?p=19051 (also https://www.markgray.com.au/gallery/limited-edition-prints/new-zealand/aoraki-mount-cook.php and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aoraki_/_Mount_Cook)
- Source: two landscape photographers' field notes from the mountain in his photograph, and the encyclopaedia entry on the name; read. The event is known from the place: "Mt. Cook is usually hidden behind clouds, many visitors leaving without the chance to even see the peak" (Gray).
- What: (1) The photograph is in the window from the first frame, sharp and in its own colours, but a cloud sits across the upper picture where the summit is: a soft-edged white and grey mass, lit from above, thin at its edges so the ridges show through them and solid at its heart. Nothing else is covered: the lake, the scrub, the window frame are all there. (2) It drifts. The nor'west wind pushes it right at a steady walking pace, its trailing edge thinning as it goes, and the summit comes out of it ridge by ridge, the way "the clouds that had been clinging to the peak" break at last (Wandering Lens). (3) The last of it leaves the right edge of the frame; the summit stands clear. (4) The stage fades on the cover. Two to four seconds; one object, one direction, one exit. On the page the cloud is a canvas or SVG mass of feTurbulence with a soft threshold, drawn once, translated across and thinned by opacity in its last third.
- Why: it is the one event every photographer at this mountain waits for, sometimes for days, and the name the old translation gave it, "cloud piercer", is the moment itself: the summit piercing the cloud as it goes. A stranger sees a mountain being uncovered by weather, which no website does, and the uncovering is partial and honest: the lake and the land were never hidden. It answers the router's note on the engine cut: something passes between the viewer and the picture, as the fence does, and the last beat is the cloud's exit, not a settling of the picture.
- Flag: nearest neighbours are Fog Line (fog lifting in place over the whole frame; here one object crossing, most of the frame never covered), Koyaanisqatsi (a cloud's shadow on the land; here the cloud itself, in front of the peak), komorebi (many small trembling shadows; here one mass, no tremble). Not pane (nothing on glass) and not a wipe (the cloud is translucent at its edges and never spans the frame). The cloud is drawn, as the drops, leaves and scraps of other finds were.
- Quality to borrow: the summit uncovered by one passing cloud that leaves the frame, the rest of the picture never hidden; weather as the thing that passes between the viewer and the mountain.

## 29. The wet plate poured: four tilts, the picture where the liquid has run
- URL: https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/amex/eastman/sfeature/wetplate_step1.html (also https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collodion_process)
- Source: PBS American Experience's step-by-step on the wet-plate process and the encyclopaedia entry; read. The gesture is described in every manual: "pour the collodion onto a glass plate, then tilt the plate until its entire surface is coated with the solution. Then pour the excess collodion back into its bottle" (PBS); the whole process has to be done "within the span of about fifteen minutes" while the plate is wet (Wikipedia).
- What: (1) A bare plate: the window is the paper, or a faint cold glass grey. A pool lands near one corner, a dark syrup with a bright meniscus. (2) The plate tilts, and the pool runs: a tongue of liquid flows to the far corner, then the plate tilts again and it runs to the next, then the third, then the fourth, four decisive tilts in about two seconds, and inside the liquid, where it has run, the photograph is there, as if the liquid carried it; where the plate is still dry there is nothing. The flow has a rounded front and leaves the corners last, so the picture arrives as four tongues joining. (3) The plate is brought level; the excess runs off one corner in a thin bright thread; the sheen of the wet surface goes matte in half a second. (4) The cover. In the real process the picture comes under the developer, poured in one flowing motion across the plate; the licence here is to let the pour that coats the plate be the pour that brings the picture, one gesture instead of two.
- Why: nobody has seen a photograph arrive by being poured. The four tilts are a rhythm with a physical reason, each tongue of liquid a decision, and the whole thing is the oldest serious way a landscape was ever photographed in the field (Watkins and Muybridge carried the glass and the tent up the Sierra). It is a photographer's gesture before it is an effect, and it ends on a level plate.
- Flag: kin to the darkroom (excluded) only in that a chemical brings the picture; there the whole print develops in place from black, grain then shapes then colour; here nothing develops, the picture is whole and sharp inside a liquid front that moves, and the dry plate shows nothing. Kin to the ink blot (scout 3) in having a liquid edge; the blot blooms from a centre, the pour runs corner to corner under tilts. Buildable: an SVG mask of four overlapping blob paths grown in sequence with feTurbulence on the edge, a specular gradient layer for the sheen, one line for the run-off. Reduced motion cuts to the cover.
- Quality to borrow: the picture arriving where a liquid runs, under four tilts of the plate, and stopping when the plate is level.

## 30. Alpenglow: first light on the summit, walking down the face, then the valley all at once
- URL: https://garyhartblog.com/tag/alpenglow/ (also https://www.lightstalking.com/alpenglow/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpenglow)
- Source: Gary Hart's field posts from the Sierra crest, a photography tutorial and the encyclopaedia entry; read. Hart: "the best light on the Sierra crest usually starts a couple of minutes before the 'official' (flat horizon) sunrise"; the tutorial: it lasts "usually between 5 to 10 minutes". The descent of the lit zone is from standing at a mountain at dawn, which every landscape photographer has done.
- What: (1) The photograph is present but in the blue of the last minutes before sunrise: cold, flat, the snow grey-blue, the lake dark, no shadows. (2) A touch of rose lands on the very summit, the highest snow only, a small warm patch against the cold mass. (3) It walks down. The lit zone descends the face, bounded by the mountain's own shape (the snowfields take it first, the rock after), warming from rose to gold to white as the sun climbs, the shadow line sliding down the ridges over about two seconds, while the valley and the lake stay in shade. (4) The sun clears the eastern ridge behind the camera: the lake, the scrub, the window frame take the light in one quick sweep, the picture is in full sun, and the stage fades on the cover. Ten minutes made three seconds; two speeds, a slow descent and a fast sweep.
- Why: it is the holiest ten minutes of a landscape photographer's day, the reason he is standing in the cold, shown once at full size; the stranger watches the day reach the mountain before it reaches them. The authored moment is the first patch of rose on the summit, which pulls the eye to the one place the photograph is about. It answers the router's Skyspace note by being a light sequence with its own beats and its own ending (if the router wants the clock, the season at Aoraki from the visitor's Date can set the first light's warmth; it is not needed).
- Flag: nearest neighbours are Lux Noctis (scout 4: a lamp's path over the land at night; here the sun, the shape of the lit zone set by the relief, two beats), the graduated ND (scout 4: a dark sheet sliding down to the horizon; here light descending the mountain only, nothing sliding, the horizon untouched until the sweep) and hand-tinting (scout 3: colour by regions in an order chosen by a hand; here one zone descending in the order gravity and the sun give). Not exposure (excluded: the whole frame from white, stop by stop). Buildable: the colour photograph under a cold-graded copy, an SVG mask whose edge follows a rough contour of the mountain (the build already has a mountain outline from the outline opening's work) and translates down, a second mask for the sweep.
- Quality to borrow: light reaching the summit first and walking down the face, the valley lighting last and all at once; the day arriving on the mountain before it arrives on the viewer.

## 31. The cap on its keeper: a pendulum through the view, each pass shorter, dying out of the frame
- URL: https://fotodioxpro.com/products/cap-trap (the quality at full scale: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botafumeiro)
- Source: a cap-keeper maker's page ("choose how far you want the cap to hang away from the lens so it won't dangle too far or get in your way") and the encyclopaedia entry on the Santiago censer; read. The event is from use: a lens cap on its tether swings into the bottom of a wide frame on the first shot after it is pulled.
- What: (1) The photograph fills the window from the first frame, still and whole. (2) From the bottom edge a dark disc on a thin cord swings up and across the lower picture, a pendulum from a pivot below the frame, crossing the lake, out at the far side; it comes back a little lower, crosses again, and again, each pass shorter and slower than the last, the way a pendulum dies: the Botafumeiro "swings in a 65-meter arc" across the transept before the congregation after "about 17 cycles" of pulling, and when the pulling stops its arcs shrink until it hangs. (3) The last arcs no longer reach the frame; a final edge of the disc shows and drops away below. (4) Stillness; the cover. Three to four seconds; an underdamped sine on the angle, four or five crossings, the disc blurred slightly at speed.
- Why: a thing passing between the viewer and the picture whose rhythm is itself the countdown: each pass shorter, so the eye knows the ending before it comes, and the last beat is the disc's exit, not any change in the picture. It is a photographer's own small accident made into the opening, and it has a sound everyone can hear without hearing it. For the router: this is the "last beat of a reveal that is not a settling" asked for after the engine cut; nothing about the photograph shivers, blurs or settles; the only moving thing is in front of it and leaves.
- Flag: kin to Masstransiscope (scout 2: a wall of slits passing at a train's pace; here one object on a pendulum, slowing) and to the self-timer (my round 2: tempo as countdown; there a blinking light on paper, here a passing object whose passes shorten). The disc is drawn, as the fence's bars were. Not pane's family: the cap never covers the picture, it crosses a strip of it and is gone. Reduced motion skips it.
- Quality to borrow: a passing object whose passes shorten until it leaves, the rhythm of a pendulum dying as the countdown; the picture arrives by being left alone.

## 32. The screen print: the flood stroke, then the print stroke that leaves the picture behind the blade
- URL: https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/s/screenprint (the two strokes: https://patents.google.com/patent/US4817524)
- Source: Tate's glossary ("ink or paint is then forced through the (non-blocked areas of) open fabric with a rubber blade, known as a squeegee, onto the paper") and a press patent's background ("the squeegee is then drawn across the mesh in a print stroke"; in the flood stroke "the squeegee is moved a certain distance above the screen, and then moved back to its starting position"; the blade "locally deforms the mesh such that the mesh is forced down into contact with substrate", the mesh lifting again behind it); read. Warhol's photographic screens made it the way a photograph becomes a print on cloth or paper.
- What: (1) The window is the screen seen from above: paper beneath, a fine mesh over it, the photograph nowhere. (2) The flood stroke: a bar crosses the window in one direction and leaves behind it a flat even coat of one colour on the mesh, the ink flooded in, so the window becomes a single flat tone, nothing readable. (3) The print stroke: the blade comes back the other way at a firm even speed, and behind it the flat tone is gone and the photograph is there, wet and glossy, pressed through onto the paper, the mesh lifting off it a hair behind the blade. (4) The blade leaves the far edge; the gloss goes matte; the cover. Two passes in opposite directions, about a second each, with a short pause between.
- Why: a reveal with two beats in two directions, the first hiding everything under one colour, the second making the picture, so the stranger is set up and then paid; it is a hand's work with a tool, at a speed a hand would use, and the picture is not uncovered but made. Screen printing is the one print process a stranger recognises at once.
- Flag: nearest neighbours are the shutter curtain (my round 2: one opaque edge sweeping once, the picture behind it from the first instant; here two strokes, the first one covering, and the picture does not exist until the second), the woodblock (scout 3: impressions landing flat, one per colour; here one pull, no stamping) and the printhead (scout 4: a machine's bands; here one blade, one pass). Not slices, not a curtain: the flat colour of the flood stroke is the site's own stone or clay and spans the window only between the strokes. Buildable: two clip-path insets driven by one timeline, a repeating-linear-gradient hairline for the mesh at low opacity, a specular layer for the wet ink that fades.
- Quality to borrow: two strokes, out and back: the first floods the window with one flat colour, the second leaves the photograph behind the blade.

## 33. The watershed flood: the picture floods in by its own relief, flat regions as sheets, the edges last
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watershed_(image_processing)
- Source: the encyclopaedia entry on the watershed transform (S. Beucher and C. Lantuéjoul, 1979; Vincent and Soille's flooding algorithm, 1991); read. "The watershed transformation treats the image it operates upon like a topographic map, with the brightness of each point representing its height"; the method is "placing a water source in each regional minimum in the relief, to flood the entire relief from sources, and build barriers when different water sources meet."
- What: (1) Blank paper. (2) Water is let in at one or a few points of the photograph's own relief (its grey levels as altitude: the smooth sky and the smooth lake are plains, the mountain's rock and the window frame are broken ground) and the picture appears wherever the flood has reached: it races across the sky as one sheet, pours into the lake as another, climbs the snowfields, and slows in the rough ground of the ridges and the scrub, which fill last, pixel by pixel, their edges the last things to come. The order and the shapes are computed from the photograph, not drawn. (3) The last dark pixels of the rock and the frame fill; the picture is whole in one instant with no fade. (4) The cover. Two to three seconds; on the page the flood is a priority-queue fill over the 640 copy, each pixel given an arrival time, and that time map drives a canvas mask at full size.
- Why: a landscape photograph treated as a landscape: the flood finds the lake because the lake is flat, and it finds the mountain hard going because the mountain is rough, so the stranger watches water read the picture, and the reading is true. It is the camera's picture measuring itself, the quality of the histogram, but spread over the whole frame as a motion with a shape nobody has seen on a website. It answers the router's anamorphosis note with a composition that arrives (regions of the picture, in an order the picture sets) and is neither pieces nor a transform undone.
- Flag: nearest neighbours are hand-tinting (scout 3: regions in an order a hand chose, colour only, over a whole monochrome picture; here the regions are found by the picture, the order by its relief, and nothing is there before the flood), the ink blot (scout 3: one liquid edge from a centre with noise on it; here many fronts, their speed set by the pixels), the histogram (my round 2: the picture's own measurement on paper, then the picture; here the measurement is the reveal). Not pixels (excluded: spatial resolution halving), not mosaic (no tiles), not darkroom (nothing develops; a pixel is absent or whole). The risk is that it reads as a paint-bucket: keep the fronts soft by a few pixels and the pace uneven, fast on the plains and slow on the rock, which the relief gives for free.
- Quality to borrow: the photograph arriving in the order its own relief sets, flat regions as whole sheets, the broken edges last; the picture read by a flood.
